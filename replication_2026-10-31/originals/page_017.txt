We automate the generation of these forecast queries and collect responses using an AI
assistant. Each query embeds a forecaster profile (education, job title, organization type, and
related characteristics), which induces heterogeneity in the simulated panel while holding the
information environment fixed. To mirror the SPF process as closely as possible, we also provide
our AI forecasters with a data environment that closely matches that of human participants.
Specifically, the prompt includes past median SPF forecasts and real-time macroeconomic data.
This setup largely ensures that any differences between human and AI forecasts arise from
differences in forecasting behavior rather than from differences in the underlying information
set. In the next section, we evaluate how closely the behavior of AI forecasters matches that of
the human SPF panel.
5
Results
This section presents our simulation results and validates them against data from human fore-
casters.
With two sets of forecasts (human and AI) for five forecasting horizons across 23
variables, the amount of output is substantial. To keep the presentation focused, we therefore
discuss results for selected horizons only, and focus mainly on four key variables in our dis-
cussion: the CPI inflation rate, 3-month Treasury bill rate, unemployment rate, and real GDP
index.15 We choose to focus on these four economic variables because they cover key aspects
of the economy: price stability, interest rates, labor market conditions, and overall economic
output. This provides a comprehensive test of the language model’s ability to simulate human
forecasting behavior across different dimensions of economic activity. Forecasts are winsorized
at the median ±3 standard deviations of the realized data to exclude potential erratic LLM
answers.16
