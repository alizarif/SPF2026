In contrast, removing real-time data often pushes the ratios further above one, showing a
clearer deterioration in how well the AI forecaster tracks realized outcomes. Eliminating both
real-time data and past median SPF forecasts is associated with even higher MAE ratios, and we
can typically reject that these ratios are equal to one with p-values below one percent. For some
variables, the loss in performance is extreme. For example, the four-quarter-ahead expectations
for real GDP are 73% worse compared with the baseline, whereas the nowcast is worse by a
factor near six. Eliminating these pieces of information forces the AI to rely almost entirely
on its pre-trained knowledge without current context or the valuable proxy for zt. In terms of
the framework, this is equivalent to conditioning only on a much more limited information set,
leading to a larger approximation error and thus much higher MAEs.
The importance of past median SPF forecasts for generating forecasts reflect the persistence
of most variables in the SPF. However, we show in Appendix D that human SPF forecasts are
not merely simple AR(1) model-implied forecasts, and that AI forecasts are closer to the human
survey compared with AR(1) forecasts.
Overall, the performance of the AI forecaster deteriorates progressively as more inputs are
removed from the prompt, highlighting that information inputs play a key role in determining
the performance of LLMs: SPF-like median expectations require common macro information
and a consensus signal. In particular, past median SPF forecasts strongly contribute to the AI’s
ability to reproduce human expectations and to track realizations. The critical role of lagged
human forecasts reflects a fundamental feature of our approach. On the one hand, conditioning
on lagged human forecasts enables the LLM to access indirect signals about factors that are
difficult to quantify (professional judgment, institutional knowledge, narrative context). On
the other hand, this dependence implies that the current LLM framework is best suited as a
complement to traditional surveys, valuable for exploring counterfactual scenarios and belief
distributions, rather than as a replacement.
