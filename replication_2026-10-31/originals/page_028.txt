Using this framework, we construct a synthetic panel of forecasters designed to mirror the
institutional environment of the Survey of Professional Forecasters. We show that the resulting
AI-generated panel reproduces salient features of the human survey, including median forecast
levels.
Importantly, while aligned in many dimensions, AI and human forecasts differ in
systematic and interpretable ways that reflect differences in information availability, information
processing, and updating behavior.
Our ablation exercises clarify why this occurs.
Performance improves notably when the
LLM is augmented with lagged human forecasts, real-time data, and forecaster characteris-
tics. Removing these inputs systematically worsens performance, with the largest deterioration
occurring when past SPF medians are excluded. Lagged human forecasts are useful because
they capture things we cannot easily measure, like judgment, context, and institutional knowl-
edge. By using these forecasts, the model can better mimic how people form expectations.
We therefore view the framework not as a replacement for human survey participants, but as a
complementary measurement tool that enables controlled analysis of how expectations respond
to alternative information environments.
A key advantage of LLM-based panels is their flexibility. Traditional surveys cannot retroac-
tively modify panel composition, control information availability, or experiment with alternative
question designs. LLM-based panels enable all of these counterfactual analyses with appro-
priate context engineering. This, in turn, allows researchers to prototype new survey designs
at minimal cost and simulate counterfactual forecast distributions for past periods under cus-
tom information sets. More broadly, our results point to a substantive role for generative AI
in macroeconomics: not as a forecasting oracle, but as a laboratory for studying expectations
formation. By combining institutional realism with flexible context engineering, the framework
allows researchers to analyze expectations in a disciplined and transparent way. We see this
approach as a step toward integrating modern machine learning tools into the measurement
of expectations in a manner that complements economic theory and existing survey-based
methods.
