To address these concerns, we systematically validate our forecaster model. We replicate our
simulations across multiple LLM architectures (GPT, Llama, and DeepSeek) and under both
deterministic and stochastic decoding in order to assess how model choice and randomness
affect the distribution of forecasts and panel behavior, and to ensure that our findings are not
driven by a particular model. We also vary the definition of the forecaster persona within a
fixed prompt structure to isolate the effect of role framing on the expectations formation rules
inferred by the model.
Our approach complements recent research on LLMs as economic agents, which suggests
that language models, and their biases, are significantly influenced by the roles they are asked
to play. In particular, Bybee (2025) demonstrates that LLMs not only simulate expectations but
also systematically replicate certain types of errors. A growing set of studies (e.g., Kazinnik,
2026; Cook and Kazinnik, 2025; Fedyk et al., 2024; Zarifhonarvar, 2026) similarly explores how
assigning specific roles to LLMs affects their expectations, decisions and behavior in economic
environments. We extend this line of research in a different direction: rather than studying
how role prompts shift behavior or expectations in stylized or single-wave environments, we
construct an institutionally grounded synthetic panel of professional forecasters that mirrors the
SPF’s repeated, multi-horizon forecasting environment.
This work also relates to the emerging literature on the application of LLMs in economic
forecasting. For example, Faria-e Castro and Leibovici (2023) show that LLMs such as Google’s
PaLM can generate inflation forecasts that often outperform the SPF. In contrast, our primary
objective is not to develop a superior forecasting tool, but to model the behavior of SPF partic-
ipants.4 The paper is the first, to our knowledge, to construct and validate an institutionally
grounded synthetic panel of individual professional macroeconomic forecasters, designed to
mirror the SPF’s repeated, multi-variable, multi-horizon survey environment. By combining
a novel, hand-collected dataset of professional forecaster characteristics with forecasts for 23
