5.5
Reasoning Patterns
Surveys of professional forecasters typically provide numerical expectations but limited evi-
dence on the reasoning behind them. A distinctive feature of LLM-based forecasting panels
is the ability to elicit reasoning alongside numerical predictions, allowing us to study how the
type of reasoning changes across macroeconomic environments. After each forecast, we prompt
the model to provide a brief rationale, generating explanations that clarify the considerations
underlying the resulting forecasts.
Since 2008, the European Central Bank has conducted special surveys at roughly five-year in-
tervals to better understand the processes and methodologies used by professional forecasters.
In this section, we use evidence from these surveys to assess the extent to which the simulated
reasoning data align with documented forecasting practices among human forecasters. Specif-
ically, we classify each reasoning response into four categories using keyword frequency rules:
Mechanical (references to trends, historical averages, and mean-reversion), Policy (references to
Federal Reserve actions, fiscal or monetary policy, and institutional responses), Model (refer-
ences to economic theory such as the Phillips curve, Okun’s law, or structural models), and
Event (references to specific shocks, crises, or salient events such as the pandemic or financial
crisis).18
Following the ECB methodology (European Central Bank, 2024), we aggregate these cate-
gories into two broader types: model-based reasoning (combining Model and Mechanical, as both
involve mathematical representations of economic relationships) and judgment-based reasoning
(combining Policy and Event, reflecting experience-driven interpretation of current conditions).
The ECB defines model-based forecasting as relying on “any mathematical representation of
relationships between economic variables” and judgment-based forecasting as relying on “ex-
perience and intuition” (European Central Bank, 2019).
