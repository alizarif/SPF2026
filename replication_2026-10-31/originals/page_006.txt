Taken together, our results suggest that LLM-based forecasters can serve as a scalable comple-
ment to traditional expectations surveys, rather than as a replacement for existing forecasting
surveys and tools. Because synthetic panels can be run continuously at negligible marginal
cost, they allow high-frequency tracking of the cross-sectional distribution of beliefs, including
disagreement and tail risks, which is especially valuable in periods of heightened uncertainty.
By flexibly varying the information set and combining structured real-time macro data with
unstructured inputs (e.g., news, policy statements, scenario descriptions), researchers and pol-
icymakers can study how modeled forecasters would revise expectations across alternative
information environments. The same framework can prototype and test new survey questions,
fill in historical responses under consistent information sets, and assess counterfactual scenar-
ios. In this sense, the LLM-based panel functions as a laboratory for expectations formation
and survey design that augments existing survey infrastructure.

modeling (ABM).2 In standard ABM applications, agents follow relatively simple, explicitly
