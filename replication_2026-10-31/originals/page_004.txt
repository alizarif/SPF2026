We next isolate the contribution of each input and modeling choice by running a set of ablation
exercises. We show that removing individual characteristics increases mean absolute errors in
predicting real GDP by about 3-39%, further excluding real time data raises errors by up to
44%, and by also omitting past SPF median forecasts produces much larger errors, exceeding
70%. These patterns indicate that our AI forecasters respond systematically to the same types
of input data and information that matter for human forecasters, and that their information sets
and heterogeneity can be manipulated in ways that are informative but infeasible in real-world
survey panels.
We interpret our results through a simple framework of human and AI expectations forma-
tion inspired by Mullainathan and Spiess (2017) and Agrawal et al. (2019). Human forecasters
combine observable variables (e.g., labor market data) with difficult-to-observe inputs such as
experience, judgment, and narrative context. Their forecasts reflect both cognitive constraints
and informational advantages. In contrast, traditional prediction algorithms can process ob-
servable data very efficiently but cannot access these latent components. Generative AI models
bridge this gap. By training on massive text corpora, LLMs internalize regularities in how
humans transform information into verbal judgments and numerical forecasts, allowing them
to approximate aspects of human expectations formation without direct access to individual
forecasters’ latent states. In our setting, we use this capability to construct LLM-based forecaster
personas, which map real-time macroeconomic data, forecaster characteristics, and past SPF
forecasts into survey responses. This design allows LLMs to combine the statistical advantages
of algorithms with a stylized representation of human judgment, generating synthetic survey
forecasts that both resemble human panel behavior and can be used to study how expectations
would change under alternative information sets or forecaster characteristics.
In addition, by incorporating individual characteristics, our framework shifts attention from
a single aggregate forecast to the cross-sectional distribution of expectations across forecasters.
