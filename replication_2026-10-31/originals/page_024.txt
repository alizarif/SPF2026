Forecast Revision Dynamics
A central question in the expectations literature is how forecasters update beliefs in response
to new information. Professional forecasts may reflect error correction, information rigidities,
attention constraints, and convergence toward consensus. Our synthetic panel allows us to ask
whether LLM-based forecasters display similar updating behavior, and where they differ from
human professionals. Human forecasters update their beliefs gradually in response to new
information and past forecast errors (Coibion and Gorodnichenko, 2012; Bordalo et al., 2020).
We assess whether AI forecasters replicate the patterns of human professional forecasters by
analyzing forecast revisions across consecutive quarters for both panels. For each forecaster i
and survey t, we define the forecast revision as:
revisioni,t = forecasti,t − forecasti,t−1,
(12)
where forecast is either the human forecast, hi,t, or the AI forecast, mAI
i,t.
We test two hypotheses central to the expectations formation literature: (1) forecasters correct
past errors, and (2) forecasters converge toward the consensus view. The first hypothesis is
assessed by regressing revisions on the forecast error from the previous survey. A coefficient
of one implies that forecasters fully revise their predictions in response to past errors; e.g., if
a forecaster underestimated GDP by $100 billion, they revise upward by approximately $100
billion in the following quarter. To test the second hypothesis, we regress forecast revisions on
deviations from the consensus, i.e., median forecast, within each panel.
Table 11 reports these regressions separately for the nowcasts of human SPF participants
(Panel A) and AI forecasters (Panel B). Both human and AI forecasters exhibit significant error
correction, but with different magnitudes.
