The AI forecaster has access to observed macroeconomic data, the past median human fore-
cast, and a set of assigned personal characteristics.
The lagged human median forecasts,
although imperfect and contaminated by biases and noise, contain information about zt be-
cause this information is available to humans. The model leverages these forecasts as proxies
for the unobserved factors. The inclusion of personal characteristics helps the model learn the
systematic patterns that vary across forecasters. This formulation emphasizes that our frame-
work augments traditional forecasting, not by substituting human judgment with algorithms,
but by integrating human-derived insights and judgment into the forecasting process. In this
sense, our framework fits into the broader agenda of combining flexible statistical models with
economically structured questions. Whited (2023) argues that reduced form and structural
methods are complements rather than substitutes, and documents several ways to use high
dimensional statistical models to inform and discipline structural analysis. In this spirit, we
treat the LLM as a rich reduced-form representation of forecasters’ expectation formation rules
that could ultimately be embedded in structural models of the economy.
Finally, we stress that AI forecasters are not given an advantage over humans in our frame-
work. On the contrary, humans have access to more information given by zt than AI forecasters,
including the potential use of sophisticated forecasting models that help them exploit patterns
in the data.
